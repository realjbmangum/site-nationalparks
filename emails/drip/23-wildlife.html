The Best National Parks for Wildlife Watching

Seeing a wild bison herd cross the road in front of you, or spotting a grizzly bear fishing in a river from a safe distance -- these are the moments that make national parks unforgettable. Here's where to go for the best wildlife encounters.

Yellowstone is the undisputed champion. Lamar Valley is called the "Serengeti of North America" for good reason. Drive the valley at dawn and you'll see bison herds, pronghorn, coyotes, and if you're lucky, wolves. Bring binoculars and pull over at the Lamar Valley overlooks between Tower Junction and the Northeast Entrance.

Grand Teton is moose country. The Willow Flats area near Jackson Lake Lodge is one of the most reliable spots in the entire park system to see moose feeding at dawn and dusk. You'll also spot bald eagles, osprey, and elk along the Snake River.

Denali gives you grizzlies, caribou, Dall sheep, and wolves across a vast tundra landscape. Take the park bus deep into the park -- private vehicles are limited to the first 15 miles, but the bus goes to Wonder Lake, where wildlife sightings are almost guaranteed.

Great Smoky Mountains is home to roughly 1,500 black bears. Cades Cove loop road is the best spot -- go early morning or late evening. In fall, elk congregate in the Cataloochee Valley, and their bugling echoes through the mountains.

Safety Tip

Always stay at least 100 yards from bears and wolves, and 25 yards from other large animals. Use a telephoto lens or binoculars instead of walking closer. If a bear changes its behavior because of you, you're too close. Carry bear spray in grizzly country.
