The Northern Rockies Triple Crown: Three Parks, One Epic Loop

Rocky Mountain, Grand Teton, and Yellowstone. Three of the most iconic national parks in the country, and they're all connected by one of the best road trip routes in the West. This is the trip that belongs at the top of your list.

Start in Rocky Mountain National Park (Estes Park, CO). Drive Trail Ridge Road, the highest continuous paved road in North America, topping out at 12,183 feet. The alpine tundra up there feels like another planet. Hike Emerald Lake Trail (3.6 miles round trip) for a postcard-perfect mountain lake framed by Hallett Peak.

Drive north to Grand Teton (about 7.5 hours via I-25 and US-191). The Teton Range rises 7,000 feet straight up from the valley floor with no foothills -- it's one of the most dramatic mountain views in North America. Paddle Jenny Lake, hike to Inspiration Point, and watch the sunrise from Schwabacher Landing.

Finish in Yellowstone, just 10 miles north of Grand Teton. You'll need at least two full days here. Hit the Upper Geyser Basin for Old Faithful and Grand Prismatic Spring, drive through Hayden Valley for bison and wildlife, and don't skip the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone -- Artist Point gives you a view of the 308-foot Lower Falls that you'll never forget.

Planning Tip

Budget 7-10 days for this loop. July and August are peak season -- book campgrounds and lodges 6 months ahead. September is quieter with fall colors and elk rut. Trail Ridge Road in Rocky Mountain typically closes mid-October through Memorial Day due to snow.
