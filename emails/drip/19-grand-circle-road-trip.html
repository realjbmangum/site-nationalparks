The Grand Circle: 6 Parks, 4 States, 14 Days

If you are going to take one big national parks road trip in your life, make it the Grand Circle. This loop through Arizona, Utah, Colorado, and New Mexico connects six national parks through some of the most jaw-dropping landscapes on the planet. Every single day of driving looks different from the last.

Start at the Grand Canyon South Rim. Watch sunrise from Mather Point, hike partway down the Bright Angel Trail, and drive the Desert View Watchtower route. Two days gives you a proper experience without rushing.

Head north to Zion. The Narrows -- wading through the Virgin River between thousand-foot canyon walls -- is unlike anything else in the national park system. Angels Landing is the most famous day hike in America for a reason, but the chains section is not for everyone. The Canyon Overlook Trail (1 mile round trip) offers a stunning alternative with zero exposure.

Just 80 miles east, Bryce Canyon is a landscape from another world. The Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden trails drop you into an amphitheater of orange hoodoos that look like they were carved by hand. Sunrise here is unforgettable.

Continue the loop through Mesa Verde in Colorado -- home to 600 cliff dwellings built by Ancestral Puebloans over 700 years ago. The Cliff Palace tour is mandatory. Then swing south through Petrified Forest in Arizona, where 225-million-year-old trees have turned to crystal, and finish at Saguaro outside Tucson for sunset among the iconic cacti.

The full loop is roughly 1,500 miles. Spring (April-May) and fall (September-October) offer the best weather and smallest crowds.

Quick Tip

Zion requires shuttle buses to access the main canyon from March through November -- no private vehicles allowed. Arrive before 8 AM to avoid long shuttle waits, or consider staying at Zion Lodge inside the park for walk-on access.
