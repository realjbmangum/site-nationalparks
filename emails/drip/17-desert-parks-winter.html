Winter is peak season for desert parks — mild temps, clear skies, no crowds.

Why Winter Is the Best Time to Visit America's Desert Parks

While most national parks are buried in snow from November through March, the desert parks are having their best season. Mild temperatures, crystal-clear skies, fewer crowds, and some of the most spectacular stargazing conditions anywhere in the country.

Death Valley is borderline suicidal in summer -- air temperatures regularly hit 130 degrees Fahrenheit. But in winter? Highs in the 60s and 70s. You can actually hike Golden Canyon, explore the Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes at sunset, and drive Artist's Drive without risking heat stroke. If rain hits at just the right time, you might even catch the rare superbloom.

Joshua Tree is a winter paradise. Daytime temps hover around 60 degrees -- perfect for scrambling through the Wonderland of Rocks, climbing at Hidden Valley, or hiking the 49 Palms Oasis trail. At night, Joshua Tree is one of the best dark sky parks in America. Winter's early sunsets mean you do not have to stay up late to see the Milky Way.

Saguaro near Tucson and Big Bend in West Texas are equally ideal. Saguaro's winter wildflower season begins in February, and Big Bend's Santa Elena Canyon is at its most dramatic when the Rio Grande runs higher in the cooler months.

While everyone else is waiting for summer, smart visitors are booking desert trips right now.

Quick Tip

Desert nights get cold -- sometimes below freezing. If you are camping, bring a sleeping bag rated to at least 20 degrees Fahrenheit. Layered clothing is essential: T-shirt at noon, down jacket by 6 PM.
