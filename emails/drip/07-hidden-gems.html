5 National Parks Most People Have Never Heard Of

Yellowstone gets 4.5 million visitors a year. The parks on this list? Some get fewer than 80,000. That means empty trails, no parking drama, and the kind of solitude that the national park system was originally created to provide.

1. Congaree (South Carolina) -- The largest intact old-growth bottomland hardwood forest in North America. Boardwalk trails weave through towering bald cypress and tupelo trees. The synchronous firefly display in late May is one of nature's most magical events -- and it's free to enter, year-round.

2. North Cascades (Washington) -- Often called "the American Alps," this park has over 300 glaciers, turquoise lakes, and serious backcountry that rivals anything in Glacier or Rainier. Diablo Lake alone is worth the drive up Highway 20.

3. Great Basin (Nevada) -- Home to 5,000-year-old bristlecone pines (the oldest living things on Earth), Lehman Caves, and some of the darkest skies in America. The Milky Way here isn't a faint smudge -- it's a blazing river of light overhead.

4. Isle Royale (Michigan) -- Accessible only by boat or seaplane, this island park in Lake Superior is the least-visited park in the lower 48. Wolves, moose, and absolute wilderness. No cars, no cell service, no crowds. Just you and the wild.

5. Dry Tortugas (Florida) -- A cluster of islands 70 miles west of Key West, reachable only by ferry or seaplane. Fort Jefferson is a massive Civil War-era fortress surrounded by crystal-clear water and incredible snorkeling. Camp on the beach under a sky full of stars.

Quick Tip

Want to check a less-visited park off your list? Congaree is the easiest to access -- it's 20 minutes from Columbia, SC, has no entrance fee, and the Boardwalk Loop Trail is flat, shaded, and only 2.4 miles.
