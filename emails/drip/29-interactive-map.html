All 63 Parks on One Interactive Map

Planning a national parks road trip usually means jumping between Google Maps, NPS.gov, a dozen blog posts, and a spreadsheet. We built something better.

Our interactive park map puts all 63 national parks on a single, zoomable map. Click any park pin to see the essentials at a glance -- name, state, entrance fee, difficulty level, top features, and a direct link to the full park page with everything you need to plan a visit.

Here's how people use it:

Road trip planning. Zoom into a region and see which parks cluster together. You might not realize that Arches, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef, Bryce Canyon, and Zion are all within a few hours of each other in Utah -- until you see them on the map. Suddenly, a one-park trip becomes a five-park road trip.

Finding parks near you. Zoom into your area and discover parks you didn't know were within driving distance. People are often surprised to find a national park just a few hours from home that they've never visited.

Dreaming and deciding. Sometimes you just want to scroll across the country and let the map inspire your next destination. Alaska, Hawaii, the Southwest desert, the Appalachian mountains -- seeing them all at once helps you decide where to go next.

Map Tip

Use the map on a desktop or tablet for the best experience -- you can zoom, pan, and click park pins more easily on a larger screen. On mobile, it works great too, but give yourself room to explore by rotating to landscape mode.
