You Might Be Closer to a National Park Than You Think

When people think "national parks," they picture the big names out West -- Yellowstone, Yosemite, Grand Canyon. But there are 63 national parks spread across 30 states, and there is a good chance one is closer to home than you realize.

California leads with 9 national parks. Alaska has 8. Utah has 5 (all of them spectacular). But you will also find parks in states you might not expect:

Ohio has Cuyahoga Valley -- a hidden gem with waterfalls, a scenic railroad, and completely free entry. Kentucky has Mammoth Cave, home to the longest cave system in the world at over 420 miles mapped. South Carolina has Congaree, with some of the tallest trees in the eastern US and a champion cypress swamp.

Michigan has Isle Royale, one of the least-visited parks in the system -- accessible only by ferry or seaplane, with wolves, moose, and zero cars. Maine has Acadia, where Cadillac Mountain is the first place in the US to see the sunrise for parts of the year.

Quick Tip

Do not overlook weekday visits to nearby parks. Even the most popular parks are dramatically quieter on Tuesday or Wednesday compared to weekends. A 2-hour drive on a Wednesday morning could give you a nearly private national park experience.
