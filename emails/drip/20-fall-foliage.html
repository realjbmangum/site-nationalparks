The 5 Best National Parks for Fall Colors

There is something about autumn in a national park that feels different from anywhere else. No power lines, no billboards, no strip malls -- just mile after mile of uninterrupted color. Here are the five parks that put on the best show.

1. Great Smoky Mountains (peak: mid-October to early November). Over 100 species of native trees means the Smokies do not just turn one color -- they turn all of them at once. Drive Newfound Gap Road for a canopy of red maples, yellow birches, and orange sassafras. Clingmans Dome offers a 360-degree panorama of color stretching into both Tennessee and North Carolina.

2. Shenandoah (peak: mid-October). Skyline Drive runs 105 miles along the crest of the Blue Ridge Mountains with 75 overlooks. In October, the entire valley below you turns into a quilt of crimson and gold. Pack a picnic and stop at every overlook that catches your eye.

3. Acadia (peak: early to mid-October). The combination of fall foliage and Atlantic coastline is unique to Acadia. Drive the Park Loop Road past Jordan Pond, Eagle Lake, and Sand Beach. The contrast of blazing orange maples against deep blue ocean and pink granite is extraordinary.

4. Zion (peak: late October to mid-November). You would not expect a desert canyon to be a fall foliage destination, but Zion's cottonwoods turn brilliant gold against the red sandstone walls. The Riverside Walk along the Virgin River is the best spot to see it -- and the crowds are a fraction of summer.

5. Grand Canyon (peak: October). The North Rim closes in mid-October, but before it does, the aspens on the Kaibab Plateau turn a shimmering gold that contrasts with the red and purple canyon walls below. Fewer than 10% of Grand Canyon visitors ever see the North Rim -- in fall, it is practically private.

Quick Tip

Fall is also shoulder season for lodging prices. Hotels and cabins near Shenandoah and the Smokies are often 30-40% cheaper in October than in peak summer. Book midweek for the best deals and empty trails.
