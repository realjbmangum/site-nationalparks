The Pacific Northwest Loop: Rainforests to Volcanoes in One Trip

The Pacific Northwest has the most ecologically diverse cluster of national parks in the country. You can stand in a temperate rainforest, summit a glacier-capped volcano, and stare into the deepest lake in America -- all within a 10-day road trip.

Start at Olympic on Washington's peninsula. This single park contains three entirely different ecosystems: the moss-draped Hoh Rain Forest, the rugged Pacific coastline at Rialto Beach, and the alpine meadows of Hurricane Ridge. Plan two full days here minimum.

Head east to Mount Rainier. At 14,411 feet, the mountain dominates the Seattle skyline -- but up close it is staggering. The Skyline Trail at Paradise is one of the best day hikes in America: 5.5 miles through wildflower meadows with the mountain towering above you.

Continue south into Oregon to Crater Lake. Formed when Mount Mazama erupted and collapsed 7,700 years ago, the lake is 1,943 feet deep and impossibly blue. Drive the 33-mile Rim Drive for views from every angle, and if you are feeling adventurous, hike the Cleetwood Cove Trail down to the water for a swim.

If you have extra days, add North Cascades -- often called the "American Alps" for its jagged peaks and over 300 glaciers. It is one of the least-visited parks in the lower 48, which means you might have entire trails to yourself.

Quick Tip

July and August are the only reliably dry months in the PNW. Crater Lake's Rim Drive typically does not fully open until July due to snow. Plan for late July through mid-September for the best conditions across all four parks.
