The Only 10 Things You Actually Need to Pack

You don't need a $3,000 ultralight setup to have a great park trip. But you do need the right essentials. After years of park trips (and plenty of mistakes), here are the 10 items I never leave home without.

1. Trail runners or hiking boots. Merrell Moab 3 or Salomon X Ultra 4. Your feet are everything out there. Break them in before your trip.

2. A real daypack (20-30L). Osprey Daylite Plus or REI Trail 25. Big enough for water, layers, and lunch. Hip belt takes weight off your shoulders.

3. Water bottle + filter. Nalgene 32oz for capacity, plus a Sawyer Squeeze filter if you'll be on longer trails. Dehydration ends more hikes than anything else.

4. Rain shell. Weather changes fast in the mountains. A packable rain jacket like the Marmot PreCip weighs 10oz and can save your entire day.

5. Sun protection. Hat, sunglasses, SPF 50. At elevation, you burn twice as fast. This isn't optional.

6. Headlamp. Black Diamond Spot 400. If a hike takes longer than expected (it will), you don't want to be on a trail in the dark.

7. First aid kit. Basic: bandages, moleskin for blisters, ibuprofen, antihistamine, medical tape. Adventure Medical Kits makes good pre-packed ones.

8. Navigation. Download offline maps in AllTrails or Gaia GPS before you go. Cell service doesn't exist in most parks.

9. Layers. Merino wool base layer + fleece midlayer. Mountain temps can swing 40 degrees between morning and afternoon.

10. Bear spray (where required). Mandatory in Glacier, Yellowstone, Grand Teton, and Denali. Rent it if you're flying -- most park gateway towns have rental shops.

Quick Tip

Don't buy everything new. REI's used gear site (re-commerce) and local gear swaps are goldmines. You can get a $180 rain jacket for $60. The gear just needs to work, not be pretty.
