The #1 Mistake New Park Visitors Make

I've watched it happen hundreds of times. Someone finally plans their dream trip to Glacier or Yosemite, drives hours to get there, and then discovers that every campsite, lodge room, and shuttle reservation has been booked solid for months.

The mistake? Not booking early enough.

National parks aren't like state parks where you can usually find a spot. The most popular campgrounds at places like Yellowstone and Glacier fill up within minutes of opening -- sometimes six months in advance. Lodges at the Grand Canyon book out a full year ahead. Even timed-entry permits at parks like Rocky Mountain, Arches, and Glacier require advance reservations during peak season.

Here's the timeline you should be working with:

12 months out: Book park lodges (Crater Lake Lodge, Old Faithful Inn, Zion Lodge).
6 months out: Reserve campsites on Recreation.gov the moment they become available.
2-3 months out: Grab timed-entry permits, shuttle tickets, and popular activity permits like Half Dome or The Wave.
2 weeks out: Check for cancellations daily -- people drop reservations constantly.

Quick Tip

Set a phone alarm for the exact date your campground reservations open on Recreation.gov. They go live at 10:00 AM ET, and popular sites like Watchman Campground in Zion sell out in under 5 minutes.
