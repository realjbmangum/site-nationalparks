How to Actually Get a Campsite

Camping inside a national park is one of the best experiences in the outdoor world. Falling asleep to the sound of the Merced River in Yosemite, waking up to elk bugling in Rocky Mountain, watching stars from your tent in Capitol Reef -- nothing compares.

But here's the hard truth: the best campgrounds sell out in minutes. Not hours. Minutes. Here's how to beat the system.

Understand the two types of sites. Most parks have both reservation-based and first-come, first-served (FCFS) sites. Reservations open on Recreation.gov exactly 6 months before your check-in date at 10:00 AM Eastern. FCFS sites require you to physically show up and claim one -- usually by 8 AM, sometimes earlier.

The reservation strategy: Create your Recreation.gov account in advance with payment info saved. Know the exact campground and dates you want. Be logged in and refreshing at 9:59 AM ET. Add to cart instantly. Don't compare sites -- grab what's available and adjust later.

The FCFS strategy: Arrive the day before you want to camp. Scout sites in the afternoon when people are checking out (usually 11 AM - 1 PM). Have your tent ready to set up immediately. Moraine Park in Rocky Mountain and Kalaloch in Olympic are great FCFS options.

The cancellation strategy: People cancel all the time. Check Recreation.gov obsessively 2-14 days before your trip. Tuesday and Wednesday mornings tend to see the most cancellations. Services like Campnab will text you when a site opens up.

Quick Tip

Don't overlook national forest campgrounds just outside park boundaries. They're cheaper ($10-20/night vs. $30+), less crowded, and often just a 15-minute drive from the park entrance. Inyo National Forest near Yosemite is a great example.
