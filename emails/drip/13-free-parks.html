7 National Parks That Won't Cost You a Dime to Enter

Most people assume every national park charges $30-35 at the gate. But several of America's best parks are completely free -- no pass, no fee, no reservation. Just show up.

Great Smoky Mountains is the most visited national park in the country with over 12 million visitors a year -- and entrance has always been free. A deed from when Tennessee donated the land requires it.

Congaree in South Carolina protects the largest intact tract of old-growth bottomland hardwood forest in the US. Free entry, incredible boardwalk trails, and bioluminescent firefly events every May.

New River Gorge in West Virginia -- America's newest national park (2020) -- offers world-class rock climbing, whitewater rafting on the Gauley River, and the iconic Bridge Walk. Zero entrance fee.

Others to know: Cuyahoga Valley (Ohio), Redwood (California), Hot Springs (Arkansas), and Voyageurs (Minnesota). Every one of them is free, every day of the year.

And even parks that do charge fees have free entrance days throughout the year -- including Martin Luther King Jr. Day, the first day of National Park Week in April, and Veterans Day.

Quick Tip

If you plan to visit 2+ fee-charging parks in a year, the America the Beautiful Pass ($80) covers entrance to every national park and federal recreation area for 12 months. It pays for itself in two visits.
